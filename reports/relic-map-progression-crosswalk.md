# Relic Map Progression Crosswalk

## Confirmed Relic structure

The official Relic rulebook establishes three concentric tiers. Outer and Middle movement uses one die and exact clockwise or anticlockwise movement without reversing direction. Special authored spaces control crossing between tiers. Inner movement changes to one space per turn in the direction printed on the space. Reaching the center leads to the scenario confrontation rather than ordinary exploration. Source: [Fantasy Flight Games Relic rulebook](https://images-cdn.fantasyflightgames.com/filer_public/73/3c/733c3e1d-ffd0-4cac-a9ef-08783b367f99/re01_relic_rulebook.pdf).

These are confirmed structural rules. The design conclusions below are inferences, not copied rules or content.

## Reusable design principles

- The safe ring teaches movement and supplies several reasons to circulate before advancing.
- Crossing tiers is a deliberate route choice anchored to authored spaces, not arbitrary adjacency.
- A movement-model change makes the Inner tier feel constrained and dangerous.
- Progression pressure rises toward a visible center objective.
- Retreat remains meaningful until the final approach.
- The scenario card owns the finale while the board owns where that finale can begin.

## Ashen Reach crosswalk

| System | Relic structure | Ashen Reach implementation | Intentional difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outer exploration | Exact die movement around Outer | 24-sector exact-distance Borderlight ring | Multiple seven transition links reduce congestion |
| Middle access | Authored crossing spaces | Seven canonical bidirectional graph edges | Crossings are edge-based; no separate edge test |
| Middle pressure | Stronger tier with exact movement | 16-sector Red March with denser Threat icons and services | Contracts, followers, and scenario preparation are integrated |
| Inner access | Restricted authored entry | Four graph links; Guardian Span route requires owner clearance | Three alternative entrances are open in standard mode |
| Inner movement | Directed one-step movement | Fixed one-step movement around eight-sector Crownfall | Ashen Reach permits either adjacent direction and authored outward exits |
| Final approach | Constrained path to center | Two origins, both requiring Gate breach note | Scenario preparation remains separate from topology |
| Center | Scenario confrontation | `center_cinder_gate` and active scenario confrontation | Center art can change without changing topology |

## Relic-feel evaluation

| Dimension | Score | Evidence and action |
|---|---:|---|
| Outer exploration | 5 | 24 continuous sectors, shops, recovery, Contracts, and seven spaced entrances |
| Transition tension | 3 | Exact movement 1 creates timing pressure; only one Inner entrance is clearance-gated |
| Middle progression | 4 | 16 continuous sectors with higher icon density and preparation functions |
| Inner danger | 4 | Fixed movement 1, severe authored text, sparse recovery, final breach trial |
| Route choice | 5 | Seven Outer–Middle and four Middle–Inner links prevent a single forced route |
| Gate clarity | 4 | Corrected Guardian text, exact lock reasons, projected transition labels |
| Challenge variety | 4 | Command, Signal, Guile, Grit, Threat, toll, and scenario challenges |
| Reward escalation | 4 | Followers, route notes, Artifacts, and final preparation increase inward |
| Return usefulness | 5 | Every cross-ring edge has an explicit reverse route |
| Center progression | 5 | Exact origins, breach clearance, stable center identity, scenario-owned confrontation |

The remaining balance question is intentional: four Middle–Inner entrances make Guardian Span a protected route rather than the universal gate. Changing that would be a topology redesign, not a correctness repair, and requires playtest approval.
