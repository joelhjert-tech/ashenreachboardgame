## Session models

- **Average session:** expect one or two migrated outcomes. Most add or remove 1 Salvage; recovery demand rises only when a Wound entry appears.
- **High-encounter session:** repeated toll/theft entries could remove 4–7 Salvage. Batch 3 needs simulation against shop prices and zero-floor frequency before approval.
- **Early game:** Salvage losses are proportionally strongest and may block the first purchase. Keep common early threats at 1 Salvage.
- **Late game:** the proposed Scar and shared tracks dominate. They should remain on rare, telegraphed branches.
- **Co-op:** two Loss Pressure and two Global Escalation proposals can accelerate defeat for everyone; each requires scenario-cap and frequency tests.
- **Rivalry:** shared-track effects can be strategically weaponized. Public attribution must not reveal private agenda information.

## Recovery and economy risks

- Four Wound proposals are recoverable but materially stronger than current runtime. Model them against shop healing and threshold recall.
- One Scar proposal preserves Scar rarity but is a severity-4 change and needs explicit content approval plus duplicate-Scar behavior.
- Nineteen Salvage treatments restore category identity for tolls, theft, and Contract rewards. Net economy may still tighten because penalty encounters are more repeatable than Contract completions.
- Equipment loss and offers use existing inventory/catalog systems but require deterministic eligibility and no-target behavior.
- Temporary one-test modifiers must use existing authoritative modifier state; otherwise those seven recommendations move to Blocked rather than spawning a new framework.

## Four-seat balance critique

- New players can understand Salvage and tests, but shared pressure and the Scar require pre-resolution warnings.
- Optimizers may intentionally enter zero-Salvage tolls; those entries are accepted as bounded floor-zero setbacks, not debts.
- Family play benefits from removing six empty clauses and retiring one incoherent entry.
- Rules-lawyer risk concentrates in cost-before-reward order, duplicate Scar handling, capped tracks, and reconnect-safe temporary modifiers.
