# Scar System Balance Review

## Method

This is deterministic content analysis, not human playtest evidence. Every canonical Scar was classified by source, trigger timing, scope, magnitude, affected subsystem, and whether its Adaptation is passive, automatic, or owner-optional. The review also exercised the typed evaluator with battle, Hazard, Anomaly, route, gate, service, assistance, escalation, and movement contexts.

The owner-optional rules are now interactively usable through the same server-derived timing and usage ledger used by the evaluator. Browser and detached Simulation Lab evidence confirms the controls expose exact effects and legal targets without changing the authored magnitude. This closes the prior accessibility uncertainty; frequency and decision-time impact still require full-session human playtesting.

## Power boundary

- No base stat is permanently raised by a Scar.
- Most numerical Burdens are -1, +1 service cost, 1 Wound, 1 Salvage, or one extra legal displacement step.
- Most Adaptations are +1 in a narrow context, one reroll, one prevention, one inspection, or one modest recovery.
- Every limited rule records its use at turn, round, battle, visit, or game scope.
- No Adaptation adds a die, copies Equipment bonuses, grants an Artifact, prevents recall unconditionally, or modifies movement probability.
- Third-Scar retirement caps long-term stacking.

## Pairing review

Flesh pairs direct physical/route harm with repair, hazard, toxin, elite, or void-specific utility. Mind pairs test/assistance/Contract pressure with information, rerolls, and focused Signal/Command recovery. Machine burdens the economy and Equipment lifecycle while granting gate, repair, Trophy, or machine-enemy efficiency. Rift narrows cooperation or destabilizes choices/movement in exchange for rerolls, inspection, or Anomaly control. Unbound has the broadest identity costs and the most conditional rewards.

No card is an unconditional net upgrade: each Adaptation is narrower than its Burden or constrained by a stricter timing/scope.

## Economy

The largest recurring costs are +1 to eligible paid services and the Gate Mark toll. Purification costs 4 Salvage and is once per arrival. Ash Hunger can grant 1 Salvage only after an elite victory while carrying at least two Wounds and only when the battle itself awarded none. Trophy reduction is floor-zero and cannot create negative Trophy value. No buy/sell, Artifact, Contract-exchange, or Salvage Ledger trigger was broadened.

## Movement and multiplayer

Split Horizon asks the authoritative graph for a second legal same-ring step; it never constructs a hidden edge. Gate Mark observes real cross-ring movement. Assistance rules use base contributions already locked by the cooperative-help authority: Bell-Deafened blocks Command help, Hollow Reflection blocks all help to its owner, and Borrowed Name reduces only the first locked contribution in scope. No private teammate data is projected.

## Threshold and removal pacing

The third Scar is intentionally terminal for the current operative after stack completion. A 4-Salvage purge is expensive relative to normal Equipment prices, preserving the pressure to choose between immediate tools and long-term survival. Because it is once per arrival and removes exactly one card, repeated surgery requires real route time.

## Deferred playtest questions

- Does Gate Mark plus a service surcharge create excessive Salvage starvation in the same session?
- Is Hollow Reflection too punitive in The Broken Concordance, where help is central?
- Does Parasite Crown exhaust Equipment often enough to dominate its once-per-battle rescue?
- Does the 4-Salvage purge appear early enough to matter but late enough to remain a sacrifice?
- Are inspection Adaptations valuable at the table without extending turns?

No numerical values were changed from this static analysis alone.
